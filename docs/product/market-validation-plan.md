# ArchMind customer discovery sprint

**Duration:** 2 weeks  
**Sample:** 10 people across drawing issuance and field use  
**Goal:** determine whether a frequent, costly, underserved drawing-revision workflow exists  
**Status:** not yet run

## 1. Research principles

- Ask about past behavior, not hypothetical interest.
- Do not pitch the product during the first conversation.
- Ask for a redacted example of the last incident or the current workaround.
- Separate verbatim quotes from interpretation.
- Record negative evidence as carefully as positive evidence.
- Do not record confidential drawings, personal data, or sensitive project information in a public repository.
- A “sounds useful” response is not demand. A concrete pilot commitment is stronger evidence.

## 2. Target sample

| Count | Participant | Main research focus |
|---:|---|---|
| 3 | Architect or design consultant | Revision issuance, register ownership, transmittals |
| 3 | Site engineer or project manager | Identifying the current revision at the point of work |
| 2 | Document controller or PMC staff | Approval, distribution evidence, audit needs |
| 2 | Contractor or subcontractor | Forwarded PDFs, printed copies, cross-company handoffs |

Recruit people with recent project experience. If the first segment has no meaningful incidents, don't fill the sample with only people who already agree with the thesis.

## 3. Outreach message

> Hi, I'm researching how construction teams manage drawing revisions between design offices and site teams. I'm not selling anything. I'd like to understand how your team handled the most recent drawing revision issue, including what tools and workarounds you use today. Would you be available for a 20-minute conversation? I'm happy to keep project details anonymous and won't ask you to share confidential files.

## 4. Interview script (20 minutes)

### Opening (2 minutes)

- Explain that this is research, not a product demo.
- Ask permission to take notes.
- Avoid collecting confidential project or personal details.

### Incident walkthrough (8 minutes)

Start with:

> **Tell me about the last time someone on your project used the wrong drawing revision. What happened and what did it cost?**

Then ask:

1. When did this happen and what type of drawing was involved?
2. How did the revision reach the person using it?
3. How was the mistake discovered?
4. What was the actual consequence: rework, delay, coordination effort, dispute, or no meaningful impact?
5. Who was responsible for issuing and distributing the correct revision?
6. What happened to old printed drawings and downloaded PDFs?

If there was no incident, ask how the latest revision was handled on the most recent project and where the process was difficult. Do not force an incident that did not happen.

### Current workflow and alternatives (5 minutes)

7. Which tools are used: email, messaging apps, spreadsheets, document-control systems, or construction platforms?
8. How do recipients verify that a copy is current?
9. How are receipt, acknowledgment, approval, and issue decisions recorded?
10. What does the current process do well?
11. What is still manual or unreliable?
12. Have you evaluated or paid for tools that solve this? Why were they adopted or rejected?

### Buyer and next step (5 minutes)

13. Who owns the problem and who approves spending?
14. How often does the problem occur?
15. What would need to improve to justify switching or adding a tool?
16. Would you share a redacted sample or test a narrowly defined workflow?
17. Who else experiences this problem and would be willing to discuss it?

Ask about willingness to pay only after understanding the problem and current spending. Do not anchor people with a proposed price too early.

## 5. Evidence scorecard

Score each dimension 0–2 and include a note. This is a lightweight decision aid, not a scientific scale.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recent incident | None recalled | Vague or old | Specific recent event |
| Consequence | No meaningful cost | Annoyance or unquantified effort | Quantified cost, delay, rework, or serious risk |
| Existing workaround | No clear workflow | Informal or inconsistent | Clear workaround with known burden |
| Unsolved gap | Existing system is sufficient | Minor gap | Specific gap existing tools do not handle adequately |
| Buyer clarity | No owner | User knows a possible owner | Budget owner and buying process identified |
| Commitment | Compliment only | Follow-up interest | Sample, scheduled pilot, buyer introduction, or paid test |

Maximum score: 12. Record scores alongside evidence; never let the total override strong contradictory facts.

## 6. Decision thresholds after 10 interviews

These are proposed thresholds for this small discovery sprint, not industry benchmarks.

### Continue to a narrow pilot

- At least 5 of 10 describe a specific recent problem with meaningful consequences.
- At least 3 identify a gap that their current workflow does not adequately solve.
- At least 2 commit to a concrete next step, such as a pilot, sample-document review, or introduction to the buyer.

### Narrow the segment or problem

The problem is real but concentrated in one role, project type, or handoff. Rewrite the product thesis around that segment and conduct five follow-up interviews before building more.

### Stop or change direction

Most interviewees report that existing tools work adequately, incidents are rare or low-cost, or nobody will commit to testing a solution. Preserve the findings and do not compensate by adding speculative features.

## 7. Two-week execution

### Week 1
- Recruit 15–20 candidates to complete 5 interviews.
- Write notes within 24 hours of each conversation.
- Update the competitor comparison only when a customer names a real alternative or a gap.
- Do not build new features.

### Week 2
- Complete 5 more interviews.
- Compare evidence by role and existing tool.
- Identify the most frequent and costly problem.
- Apply the decision thresholds.
- Produce a one-page decision: continue, narrow, or stop, with evidence and unresolved assumptions.
