# Competitor landscape: drawing revision control

**Status:** desk research, not a complete competitive analysis  
**Last checked:** 2026-10-20

## Executive summary

QR-based verification of current drawing revisions is already a product category. ArchMind should not position “scan a QR code to see whether a drawing is current” as a unique feature.

Several products already advertise QR verification, drawing registers, revision histories, comparison, or broader construction document control. Feature availability, plan limits, regional support, and integration details should be verified directly before making buying decisions.

## Possible gaps to investigate, not claims to market

1. **Cross-company handoff:** does a workflow break when the issuer and receiver use different systems?
2. **Email and messaging circulation:** can the recipient establish current status when the PDF was downloaded or forwarded outside the primary platform?
3. **Distribution evidence:** is there a meaningful need to know who received or acknowledged a revision, and is that missing from the customer's current system?
4. **Low-friction onboarding:** do smaller teams avoid existing platforms because of cost, setup, or access constraints?
5. **Interoperability:** do customers need a neutral record across several document-management systems?
6. **Audit reconstruction:** can a team reconstruct which source file, decision, approval, and issue event applied at a point in time?

These are research questions. F5 Scan and LatestDrawing already advertise some cross-platform, no-login, or distribution-adjacent capabilities, so none should be presented as unique without direct verification.

## Interview comparison checklist

For each interviewee, record:

- Current system(s) and paid plan, if known.
- How drawings enter and leave those systems.
- How field staff verify current revision for printed and downloaded copies.
- What is missing from the existing workflow.
- Frequency and cost of a recent failure.
- Whether a workaround already solves the issue.
- Who buys and who uses the solution.
- What evidence would justify a switch.
- Whether they will test a specific workflow using redacted documents.

## Positioning rule

Do not compete on “we have QR.” Compete only on a validated customer outcome that current tools do not deliver adequately. If no such outcome appears after ten interviews, do not expand the feature set to manufacture one.
